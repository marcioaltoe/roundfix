# One door for fleet knowledge

Knowledge the fleet produces dies in three ways today: observations live only
in conversations until a commit that Active Runs block, cross-project feedback
travels by hand, and findings pile up — 63 in this repository, 111 across
eight projects — with no lifecycle stamped, no consolidation, and no archival.
This Spec gives every observation one durable door, a triage that lands it in
the contract-true home, a rollup that keeps the active set bounded, and a
path for acquired research knowledge to reach the Secondbrain's curated
layer. The full exploration, scoring, council dissent, and chosen hybrid
direction are recorded in [the idea](./_idea.md); the measured evidence is
the adopted investigation in
[references](./references/2026-08-06-findings-accumulate-faster-than-they-become-specs.md).

## Project Constraints

- Identifier strategy: not applicable — inbox entries are named by date and
  slug, and address destinations by existing project names; `origin` and
  `destination` are declared values read as-is, and no project-owned Internal
  Identifier is minted. Source: `docs/agents/domain.md`.
- Authentication and HTTP: not applicable — the whole flow is local
  filesystem and git; no endpoint, credential, or transport is created, and
  the Secondbrain guide's secret prohibitions continue to govern every read.
  Source: `docs/agents/agent-instructions.md`.
- Active ADR obligations: applicable — ADR-0095 is the decision this Spec
  implements: the Secondbrain inbox as the fleet's single capture door, with
  sibling repositories and only the capture layer living in the brain.
  ADR-0092 owns the typed backlog this Spec routes triage into, and its
  boundary holds: triage mints typed intent, and evidence never becomes
  intent. ADR-0093 governs every check this Spec adds — declarations and
  citations only, never inference — and ADR-0094 keeps those checks
  presence-aware, so a repository without rollups or archived findings skips
  instead of failing. ADR-0081 sanctions the deterministic digest fallout of
  the authorized module and skill edits. ADR-0080 owns QA verdict semantics
  and ADR-0091 owns the authored QA gate as a typed Task node, under which
  this Spec's own graph is authored. Source: `docs/agents/domain.md`.
- Tooling authority: applicable — express maintainer authorization: granted
  2026-08-06 in the write-prd authorization gate, recorded at
  `docs/workflow/authorizations/2026-08-06-fleet-knowledge-door.md`; bounded
  files: `.agents/skills/write-idea/**` and `.agents/skills/write-prd/**`
  with their `skills/**` mirrors regenerated by `make skills-sync`,
  `internal/baseline/assets/modules/context-workflow.json`,
  `internal/baseline/assets/modules/secondbrain.json`, and
  `docs/agents/docs-layout.md` with `docs/agents/secondbrain.md` as adoption
  of the regenerated managed-block postimages. Deterministic digest fallout
  is sanctioned by ADR-0081. Three surfaces are outside this grant and are
  executed under their own contracts as recorded external dependencies: the
  maintainer's global knowledge-workspace skill, the maintainer's session-end
  hook configuration, and the secondbrain repository's inbox carve-out and
  qmd collection. Source: `docs/agents/agent-instructions.md`.

## Goals

- An observation born anywhere in the fleet is durable within a minute of
  capture and reaches its destination without ever touching a checkout an
  Active Run owns.
- `docs/findings/` holds only live work: at most fifteen active findings
  after the legacy sweep, every one carrying its lifecycle, with rollups as
  the readable map of what was learned.
- Acquired knowledge compounds: project evidence lands as triaged artifacts
  in the destination repository, and research knowledge reaches the
  Secondbrain's curated intake instead of dying with the session.
- Every rule this Spec adds is mechanically checkable; a clause without a
  check does not ship.

## User Stories

1. As a session in any fleet project, I want to capture an observation for
   another project through one door, so that it survives my session and
   never disturbs the destination's Active Runs.
2. As a session of the destination project, I want to triage my pending
   inbox into Findings or Backlog Entries committed by my own project, so
   that evidence and intent land in their contract-true homes with
   provenance preserved.
3. As the maintainer curating the fleet, I want rollups that consolidate
   related findings and license their archival, so that the active set stays
   bounded and the map of what was learned stays readable.
4. As a session running the authorial skills, I want the pending inbox read
   at the start of idea and PRD work, so that queued knowledge feeds the
   decision instead of being rediscovered or lost.
5. As a session that performed substantive external research, I want the
   digest and its sources captured for the Secondbrain with a duplicate
   check first, so that acquired knowledge becomes the base for future
   decisions instead of staying in one conversation.
6. As the maintainer, I want each session's close to draft capture entries
   automatically, so that observations stop dying in conversations that
   nobody transcribes.

## Core Features

1. The Secondbrain inbox is the fleet's single capture door: an entry is
   born committed under the destination's namespace, carrying origin,
   destination, a type hint, and its creation date. Valid destinations are
   exactly the projects the brain already mirrors, plus the brain itself;
   no new configuration exists.
2. Capture is durable and non-interfering by construction: the entry is
   committed in the brain at the moment of capture, and no file touches any
   project checkout until triage — an Active Run can never be disturbed by
   capture, and a Batch commit can never absorb it.
3. Triage runs in a session of the destination project: each pending entry
   becomes a Finding when it is evidence or a Backlog Entry when it is
   intent, committed by the destination alone; the consumed entry is moved
   to the triaged area with a pointer to the artifact it became.
   Re-routing a misaddressed entry is a move between inbox namespaces.
4. The rollup is a first-class artifact: it consolidates related findings,
   names what supersedes them, and is the license for their archival. It is
   the human-readable front page of what a repository has learned.
5. Findings gain an archival home symmetric with Specs: a finding archives
   only when its lifecycle is closed and a rollup or a Spec has absorbed
   it; the mirror preserves archived findings, so the brain keeps history.
6. One legacy sweep, rollup-first: the first rollups are written, every one
   of the 63 legacy findings receives its lifecycle status, absorbed
   findings archive, and anything no rollup references defaults to
   deferred — reviewable in a single diff. The active set ends at fifteen
   or fewer.
7. The authorial skills route through the door: idea and PRD work begins by
   reading the repository's pending inbox, and a finding whose lifecycle
   closes spawns the typed Backlog Entries its actions call for.
8. Session-end capture drafts inbox entries automatically for the
   observations the session surfaced, marked as automatic drafts, always
   pending triage, and never self-triaged.
9. Research capture: a session that performed substantive external research
   captures the digest with its sources for the brain's own namespace,
   running an advisory duplicate check first — a strong match extends the
   existing knowledge instead of duplicating it. Ingestion into the curated
   wiki remains the brain's own contract; this Spec only feeds its queue.
10. Every rule above is mechanically enforced through the existing
    consistency-check infrastructure: an active finding without its
    lifecycle, a rollup naming an unresolvable member, an archived finding
    without its license, or an invalid inbox entry is a reported failure,
    not advice.
11. The vocabulary exists before the rules use it: Inbox Entry, Rollup, and
    Triage enter the project glossary beside Finding and Backlog Entry,
    each with the names to avoid.

## User Experience

The door is invisible when it works: a session captures in one gesture and
moves on. The three moments an operator actually sees are triage — a short
queue, oldest first, each entry resolving to exactly one artifact or one
discard with a reason — the rollup as the first page read when asking "what
do we know about this area", and the pending queue answering "what is
waiting for this repository" in one listing or one search. Empty states say
what they mean: an empty inbox is rest, an empty findings directory after
the sweep is health, and a rollup with no open members is a candidate for
its own closure.

## Non-Goals / Out of Scope

- A CLI command for the inbox — contract before tool; skills and rules
  prove the shape first.
- Automated graduation into the wiki — the V2 stretch; it needs the brain's
  own write contract.
- Remote transport (issue forms, other machines) — the V1 is local-first.
- Any change to the mirror export mechanics — the existing sync closes the
  loop untouched.
- Re-typing the backlog or re-litigating its boundary — ADR-0092 stands as
  is.
- Retention automation inside the brain (sweeping triaged entries) — policy
  stated, execution manual until it hurts.
- Migrating the wiki, raw corpus, or any brain layer other than creating
  the inbox.

## Success Metrics

- Active findings after the sweep: at most fifteen, every one carrying its
  lifecycle.
- Capture to durability: under one minute, audited in the pilot.
- Inbox entries pending beyond fourteen days: zero.
- Entries with valid origin and destination: all of them.
- Run interference from capture (preflight refusals, Batch absorption):
  zero occurrences.
- Substantive-research sessions that captured their digest with sources:
  all of them, audited in the pilot.
- The save–retrieve–triage cycle proven end to end by the pilot before any
  clause binds the fleet.

## Decisions

- The Secondbrain inbox is the single capture door; projects hold only
  triaged artifacts. See ADR-0095.
- Only the capture layer lives in the brain; findings and backlog stay in
  destination repositories, so the spec pipeline never depends on an
  external repository.
- The destination always owns the commit; origin is frontmatter, never
  commit authorship.
- A rollup licenses archival; lifecycle status alone never does.
- Session-end capture ships in V1 by the maintainer's decision, over the
  council's recorded dissent — both positions preserved in the idea.
- Research capture runs an advisory qmd duplicate check before inclusion;
  on a strong match it extends rather than duplicates.
- Zero new configuration: the brain's mirrored projects are the destination
  registry.
- The pilot precedes the rules: the save–retrieve–triage cycle is proven
  with this session's own material before the clauses bind the fleet.

## Open Questions

- Triage cadence: fourteen days is a metric target — harden into a clause?
  Default: metric only, until the pilot measures real cadence.
- Does session-end capture run on every session or opt in? Default: every
  session, entries marked as automatic drafts.
- The qmd collection for the inbox: name and scope follow the brain's own
  contract when that change lands there.
- Origin-name aliasing if a repository basename ever diverges from its
  project name. Default: none until it happens.
